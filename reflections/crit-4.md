## 1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

_Your answer here._

Things that actually happened this week, if any of them is the one:

- Deciding the instrument would have **no unlock screen at all**. Browsers
  won't make sound before a gesture, and the standard "click to start" answer
  is exactly the wall clause 4 forbids. Making *every* interaction the unlock —
  and shipping a beat already on the grid — turned a platform restriction into
  the invitation. Everything else about how the page opens followed from that.
- Realising **four of the nine clauses were testable and five were not**, and
  that writing tests for the other five would have been theatre.
- The swing constant: reasoning about where a triplet actually sits, rather
  than sliding the control until it stopped sounding wrong.

## 2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

_Your answer here._

The week's premise is worth arguing with rather than just quoting: *an agent
can build a synth but can't hear the result, so your ear is the harness.* Some
angles, if they're useful —

- Every clause the agent could check, it checked. Every clause it couldn't —
  is it expressive, would a stranger play it, does it *sound* like drum and
  bass — needed a person. Is that division the interesting part of the job now,
  or the part that shrinks?
- The strongest correction this week wasn't a retry, it was widening a test
  that was policing comments instead of code. Where else are you obeying a
  sensor that measures the wrong thing?
- What did you actually change after listening, and would you have caught any
  of it by reading the diff?
